# FACTS.md: claims in the script and where they come from

Each `[F#]` in `SCRIPT.md` has an entry below.

## How this was checked (read this first)

This pass was done in a cloud container with restricted network access. Wikipedia, Wikimedia, USHMM, Yad Vashem, the Auschwitz Museum, archive.org, the Library of Congress and NARA are all blocked, and the page reader tool failed. So I **could not open the primary texts themselves** (the report, the memoir) and read them end to end. Every claim was cross-checked through web searches, which return summaries of the pages listed under each claim, and against my own knowledge of the standard literature.

That is weaker than reading the books. Before a public YouTube release, the claims marked **Check** should be confirmed against the physical memoir or report. They are mostly page-level details. None of them changes the story.

### Status labels

- **Agreed:** consistent across several independent sources found.
- **Disputed:** sources differ. The script's wording is chosen to be true across the range, or it says the matter is disputed.
- **Check:** supported by one source, or by sources close to Vrba. Confirm against the memoir or report before release.
- **Interpretation:** narrative framing rather than a factual claim. The reason it is defensible is given.

## Cold open

**F1.** In April 1944 two young men hid inside a stack of timber at Auschwitz-Birkenau while the camp searched for them.
Sources: MEM, GIL, BRIT, FRE. **Agreed.** Details are in F29–F33.

**F2.** "They want to live. And they want to warn the world before the next trains arrive."
Sources: HMD and RV give Vrba's stated motives: to warn of the planned deportation of Hungary's Jews, and to survive. MEM. **Interpretation**, based on his own account of his motives.

## 1 · Slovakia, 1942

**F3.** Born Walter Rosenberg in 1924 in Topoľčany.
Sources: BRIT, HMD, WP; RV gives 11 September 1924. **Agreed.** HMD adds that the family moved to Jaklovce in 1926 and later lived in Trnava. The script names only his birthplace.

**F4.** In 1939 Slovakia broke away from Czechoslovakia (14 March 1939) and became a separate state under a nationalist regime allied with Nazi Germany.
Sources: UPN, USHMM, WP "The Holocaust in Slovakia". **Agreed.** The regime was the clerical-nationalist Slovak People's Party under Jozef Tiso. The script says "nationalist regime" to stay simple.

**F5.** He was expelled from his Bratislava school at fifteen, worked as a labourer and studied on his own.
Sources: HMD (barred from the Bratislava gymnasium at 15; labourer; studied chemistry, English and Russian at home), WP, MEM. **Check.** One search summary says some sources give 17 rather than 15. Confirm the age in MEM.

**F6.** The Jewish Code of September 1941 ran to "nearly three hundred paragraphs", was modelled on the Nuremberg Laws, and pushed Jews out of public life.
Sources: UPN; Slovak Spectator (2021 government apology); EHRI. Regulation 198/1941 has 270 paragraphs. **Disputed (date only):** most sources give 9 September 1941, and the 2021 Slovak government statement gives 8 September. The script says only "September 1941".

**F7.** Deportations began in March 1942 (25 March). The Slovak state organised the trains and paid Germany 500 Reichsmarks per deported Jew.
Sources: UPN, AM, WP "The Holocaust in Slovakia". **Agreed.**

**F8.** About 58,000 people had been deported by October 1942.
Sources: WP (57,628 in 57 transports, March to 20 October 1942), UPN, AM (about 58,000). **Agreed.** The script says "about fifty-eight thousand".

**F9.** At 17 he decided to try to reach England and join the Czechoslovak army in exile.
Sources: HMD (early March 1942, to Britain via Hungary), BRIT, MEM, WP. **Agreed.**

**F10.** He tried to cross into Hungary. It ended in arrest, he was held at Nováky, slipped out, and was recaptured.
Sources: HMD, WP, RV, MEM. **Disputed:** some accounts say he reached Budapest and was arrested on his way back into Slovakia; others say he was stopped at the Hungarian border. The script's "He tried to cross into Hungary. It ended in arrest." is true under both versions. One account names Josef Knapp as his companion when he got out of Nováky; the script names no one.

## 2 · Majdanek, then Auschwitz

**F11.** Deported to Majdanek, on the edge of Lublin, on 14 June 1942.
Sources: WP, HMD, RV. **Agreed.**

**F12.** Arrived at Auschwitz on 30 June 1942 and became prisoner 44070.
Sources: WP, HMD, RV; MEM's later edition is titled *44070*. **Agreed** on the date and number. **Check:** one summary says the number was tattooed only on his later transfer to Birkenau. The script says only that he "became prisoner number 44070", which is true either way.

**F13.** Birkenau lay a few kilometres from the main camp and was the largest killing centre the SS built.
Sources: USHMM "Auschwitz". Auschwitz I and II are about 3 km apart. **Agreed.**

**F14.** Most people on the Jewish transports were never registered and were killed within hours.
Sources: USHMM "Auschwitz", PIP. **Agreed.** The share selected for work varied by transport. HMD quotes Vrba's own estimate of about 90% sent straight to the gas chambers; modern estimates for many transports are lower (roughly 70–80%). The script says "most" and gives no number.

## 3 · Kanada and the ramp

**F15.** From August 1942 he worked in "Kanada", named by prisoners after a country imagined as rich.
Sources: RV (Kanada work from August 1942 to June 1943); WP "Kanada warehouses"; remember.org. **Agreed.**

**F16.** Kanada sorted deportees' belongings. The deportees had been told they were being resettled. Most of it was shipped to Germany.
Sources: USHMM, WP "Kanada warehouses", MEM. **Agreed.** The list of items in the script is generic and typical of the sources, not an inventory.

**F17.** He also worked at the ramp. People had to leave their luggage, and were divided in minutes: a few to work, the rest (the old, the sick, mothers and children) to the gas chambers.
Sources: RV ("Vrba testifies": night work at the ramp outside the camp areas), MEM, USHMM "Auschwitz" (selection). **Agreed.** In 1942–43 this was the old "Judenrampe" between Auschwitz I and Birkenau. The script just says "the ramp".

**F18.** In the summer of 1943 he became a registrar (clerk) in the quarantine section of Birkenau.
Sources: RV (summer 1943, men's quarantine camp), mei1940.org (June 1943), bionity/WP (section BIIa). **Agreed** on the role. **Disputed** on whether it was "June" or "summer"; the script says "summer".

**F19.** The post gave him freedom of movement and a view of arriving trains and trucks.
Sources: mei1940.org, MEM, FRE. **Agreed.**

**F20.** He counted from memory and kept no notes.
Sources: RV and MEM (the report and its maps were from memory; the escapees carried no papers). **Agreed.**

## 4 · Hungary

**F21.** In spring 1944 Hungary had the largest Jewish community left in Europe, roughly three quarters of a million people. Its government had until then resisted German demands to deport them.
Sources: USHMM "German Troops Occupy Hungary" (760,000–780,000 on the eve of occupation; the largest surviving Jewish population in Europe); USHMM "Hungary before the German Occupation" (825,000 under Hungarian control in 1941). **Agreed.**
**Nuance not in the script:** before 1944 Hungary was not safe for Jews. In 1941 it expelled some 18,000 "alien" Jews to Kamenets-Podolsk, where most were murdered, and tens of thousands of Jewish men died in forced labour service. The script's claim covers only Kállay's refusal to hand Jews over to German deportation.

**F22.** German troops occupied Hungary on 19 March 1944.
Sources: USHMM. **Agreed.**

**F23.** Prisoners were extending the railway through the Birkenau gate so trains could run into the camp near the crematoria.
Sources: HMD (work on the siding began 15 January 1944), GIL, USHMM. The spur was completed in May 1944. **Agreed.**

**F24.** Vrba later recalled SS men joking about "Hungarian salami".
Sources: HMD, which quotes Vrba: "the SS were talking about 'Hungarian Salami' and 'A million units'". MEM. **Check:** confirm the exact wording in MEM. The script attributes it ("Vrba later recalled").

**F25.** In March 1944 he tried to warn the Theresienstadt families in the neighbouring camp, and on the night of 8 March nearly 3,800 of them were murdered.
Sources: WP "Fredy Hirsch" and "Theresienstadt family camp" (Vrba told Hirsch on 6 and 8 March and urged an uprising); CZE. **Disputed (count):** 3,791 or 3,792. The script says "nearly three thousand eight hundred". The family camp was section BIIb, next to BIIa. The killings took place on the night of 8–9 March.

**F26.** "A warning had to reach people before they boarded the trains. Someone had to get out."
**Interpretation.** It matches Vrba's stated aim of warning Hungary's Jews. I found no source that ties his escape decision directly to the family camp murders. The script places the two side by side but does not say one caused the other.

## 5 · The woodpile

**F27.** Birkenau had two rings of guards. When a prisoner went missing, the outer ring stayed in place day and night for three days while the area between was searched; then the guards were stood down.
Sources: GIL (cordon around both perimeters, with roll calls and searches for three days and nights), MEM, BRIT. **Agreed.** The script simplifies this: the inner ring was the watchtowers and fences of the living camps, and the outer ring (the *große Postenkette*) was normally manned only while prisoners worked.

**F28.** Alfred Wetzler: Slovak, six years older than Vrba, a prisoner since April 1942.
Sources: WP "Alfréd Wetzler" (born 10 May 1918 in Trnava); RV (arrived 13 April 1942, prisoner 29162). **Agreed.**

**F29.** The hiding place was a timber pile for new barracks in the construction zone called "Mexico" (BIII), with a hollow prepared with other prisoners' help.
Sources: GIL (boards stored as building material for the camp extension in Bauabschnitt III), RV, MEM. "Mexico" was the prisoners' name for BIII. **Agreed** in substance. **Check:** the helpers are named in MEM; the script does not name them, because the names could not be confirmed. Vrba places the pile roughly 300 m east of Crematorium V.

**F30.** On 7 April 1944 they climbed in, the boards were slid over them, and tobacco soaked in petrol was scattered to confuse the dogs.
Sources: BRIT (hid on 7 April; "tobacco soaked in gasoline"), GIL ("Russian tobacco, pre-soaked in petrol"), PBS. **Agreed.** One source says kerosene; most say petrol or gasoline. The script says "petrol" and uses the passive voice, because sources differ on whether the two men or their helpers scattered it.

**F31.** They were missed at evening roll call, and the sirens began.
Sources: GIL, MEM, FRE. **Agreed.**

**F32.** Three days and nights in the hollow, with search parties and dogs close by.
Sources: GIL, BRIT ("three nights and four days"), MEM. **Agreed.**

**F33.** On the evening of 10 April they heard the order to stand the guards down, and left that night.
Sources: GIL (by the evening of 10 April the cordon was withdrawn and the search called off; the pair left that night); MEM (they heard the guards being called off). **Disputed:** one source dates the withdrawal to 11 April. **This is also the main date disagreement in the story**: many sources give the escape date as 7 April (when they hid) and others as 10 April (when they left the camp). The script gives both, as "hid on the 7th, left on the night of the 10th", which is consistent with both.

**F34.** They walked south over more than 100 km, mostly at night, with little food and no papers, for eleven days.
Sources: BRIT (11 days), GIL, slovakiana.sk, Vrba–Wetzler Memorial. **Disputed:** the distance is given as about 100 km, 131 km or 140 km (the 2014 commemorative route), so the script says "more than a hundred kilometres". On the eleven days: Britannica says 11 days until they reached Žilina, but 10 April to 21 April (the border crossing) is itself 11 days, and Žilina came on about 24 April. The script attaches "eleven days" to the walk to the border, which fits the dates.

**F35.** They crossed the border near Skalité on 21 April 1944.
Sources: slovakiana.sk (between Milówka and Skalité), GIL (night of 21 April). **Check:** the exact date should be confirmed in MEM or GIL; not every summary found gave it.

**F36.** A farmer put them in touch with helpers, and they were taken to the Jewish Council in Žilina.
Sources: slovakiana.sk (a farmer; then Čadca, where they were helped by the Jewish doctor Arpád Pollák); WP; MEM. **Agreed** in outline. The farmer's name (Ondrej Čánecký in some accounts) could not be confirmed and is left out.

## 6 · The report

**F37.** On 25 April in Žilina they began to dictate. They were questioned separately and their accounts cross-checked, including against the Council's records of deportations from Slovakia.
Sources: MEM via WP (dictated separately on 25 April 1944); RV; KÁR. **Check:** the cross-check against Slovak deportation records is described in MEM and GIL. Confirm the wording there. Some sources say testimony ran from 25 to 28 April and the report was finished on 27 April.

**F38.** The report covered the camp layout from memory, the gas chambers and crematoria, and transports country by country.
Sources: REP. **Agreed.** This is visible in the published WRB text: hand-drawn plans, a description of the crematoria, and a chronological list of transports with origins.

**F39.** The report was written in Slovak and translated into German.
Sources: WP; KÁR (the Slovak original was not preserved); RV. Oskar Krasňanský translated it, with Gisela Steiner. **Agreed.** **Disputed (not in script):** the length is given as 32, 33 or 40 pages, so the script gives no page count.

**F40.** It estimated 1,765,000 Jews murdered at Birkenau in about two years (April 1942 to April 1944).
Sources: REP (country-by-country table totalling about 1,765,000, called "conservative"); RV. **Agreed** that this is the report's figure.

**F41.** Historians today put the toll for Auschwitz's whole existence at about 1.1 million, nearly a million of them Jews.
Sources: PIP (at least 1.1 million murdered, about 960,000 of them Jews; about 1.3 million deported there); USHMM. **Agreed.**
**Disagreement worth knowing:** the two figures cover different things. One is Jews in Birkenau over two years; the other is all victims over the camp's whole existence, 1940–45, including the roughly 320,000 Hungarian Jews murdered after the report was written. Since even the whole-history figure is lower than the report's two-year figure, it is fair to say the report's figure "was too high". Vrba defended his numbers for the rest of his life.

**F42.** "The core of what the two men described has been confirmed many times over."
Sources: KÁR (assesses the report's reliability; finds its account broadly accurate, with errors in detail); later testimony and documents (the Sonderkommando manuscripts, the Höss testimony, the Auschwitz Album, Nuremberg). **Agreed.** It is wording, not a quantified claim. Known errors in detail include parts of the gas-chamber description and some transport estimates.

**F43.** The report warned that Hungary's Jews were next.
Sources: WP "Vrba–Wetzler report"; RV; MEM. **Agreed.** Vrba always said the warning about Hungary was central. **Check:** some historians note that the written report itself says little explicitly about Hungary, and that the warning was mainly passed on orally to the Slovak Jewish leaders. Confirm in REP and KÁR. If the written text proves thin on this, change the line to "The two men carried a warning: Hungary's Jews were next."

**F44.** Within days, copies were on their way to Jewish leaders in Budapest.
Sources: RV and BAU (Kasztner received a copy on or about 28 April 1944; Bauer says he passed one to Géza Soós almost at once); WP "Working Group". **Disputed:** the exact date of receipt, and by whom. Krasňanský's own account differs, and Hansi Brand denied it. "Within days, copies were on their way" avoids naming a date.

**F45.** Mass deportations from Hungary began in mid-May 1944.
Sources: USHMM; Anne Frank House timeline (15 May). **Agreed.** Smaller transports left in late April; the mass deportations began on 14–15 May.

**F46.** In about eight weeks, some 437,000 people were deported, nearly all to Auschwitz, and most were murdered on arrival.
Sources: USHMM (about 437,000, 15 May to 9 July); kgerbner/Braham ("nearly 440,000 in more than 145 trains"); PIP; BRA. The exact figure, 437,402, comes from German plenipotentiary Veesenmayer's reports. **Agreed.** The script says "some" and "about".

**F47.** In June the report reached Switzerland, and its findings appeared in the Swiss press.
Sources: WP "Vrba–Wetzler report" and "Auschwitz Protocols"; RV; jewishvirtuallibrary.org. It reached Switzerland via Miklós Krausz in Budapest, Florian Manoliu of the Romanian legation and George Mantello, with wide Swiss coverage from late June 1944. **Agreed.**

**F48.** Appeals and warnings reached Horthy from the Pope, the King of Sweden and Washington.
Sources: BRA; USHMM "The Holocaust in Hungary" (pressure from the Vatican, neutral states and the Allies, and threats of war-crimes trials). Pius XII's open telegram was dated 25 June 1944 and Gustaf V's appeal 30 June 1944. US warnings went through Switzerland in June. **Check:** confirm those dates in BRA. The script gives no dates.

**F49.** On 7 July Horthy ordered the deportations stopped, and the last trains left two days later.
Sources: USHMM (7 July); WP "Alfréd Wetzler" (ordered on 7 July, stopped two days later). **Disputed:** sources give 6, 7, 8 or 9 July. Horthy told his ministers on 26 June, Hungarian sources give 6 July for the decision, the formal halt is usually given as 7 July, and the last trains ran on 9 July. The script uses the most common version.

**F50.** Historians debate how much weight the report carried, but it was part of the pressure that stopped the trains before Budapest, where some 200,000 Jews were still living.
Sources: WP (credits the publication of the report in June 1944 with helping persuade Horthy); jewishvirtuallibrary.org ("one of the factors"); BRA (multiple factors, including the military situation, the bombing of Budapest on 2 July and the intercepted Lichtheim cable). **Disputed by nature.** The script's wording ("part of the pressure", "historians still debate") deliberately avoids the popular but contested claim that the report alone "saved 200,000 lives". About 200,000 Jews were in Budapest in July 1944 (USHMM).

**F51.** Many of them survived the war, though thousands more were murdered that winter after Hungarian fascists seized power.
Sources: USHMM "The Holocaust in Hungary". The Arrow Cross took power on 15 October 1944. Thousands were shot in Budapest, many on the Danube embankment, and tens of thousands were sent on death marches toward Austria. Roughly 100,000–120,000 Jews survived in Budapest until liberation in January–February 1945. **Agreed.** "Thousands more" understates rather than overstates the toll.

**F52.** The report did not reach the families in the provincial ghettos.
Sources: BAU; RV; WP. The Hungarian Jewish Council and Kasztner did not spread the report among Jews in the provinces. **Agreed** that it did not reach them in any meaningful way. Why it did not is the disputed part (see F62 and F63).

**F53.** The Allies were asked to bomb the railway lines to Auschwitz, and they refused.
Sources: MED; jewishvirtuallibrary.org (McCloy's letter of 14 August 1944); GIL. Assistant Secretary of War John J. McCloy rejected requests on 26 June, 4 July and 14 August 1944. **Agreed.** Whether bombing would have worked is a separate, long-running debate that the script does not enter.

**F54.** Allied aircraft had already photographed the camp, and no one recognised what was in the pictures.
Sources: SAMH (4 April 1944: Lt Charles Barry of 60 Squadron, South African Air Force, photographed the area while targeting the IG Farben plant at Monowitz); BRU (analysed in 1978, published in 1979; the 1944 interpreters were not tasked with the camp, and the gas chambers could not have been identified with 1944 techniques). USHMM holds the series (missions from 4 April 1944 to 14 January 1945, courtesy of NARA). **Agreed.**

## 7 · After

**F55.** After the escape Walter Rosenberg became Rudolf Vrba, and kept the name.
Sources: BRIT, WP. **Check:** the common account is that the Slovak Jewish Council gave him false papers under that name and he later adopted it legally. I could not confirm the papers detail, so the script does not include it.

**F56.** That autumn he fought with the partisans in the Slovak National Uprising.
Sources: WP; BRIT; HMD. He joined a partisan unit in September 1944. **Agreed.**

**F57.** He studied chemistry in Prague and earned a doctorate.
Sources: WP (Czech Technical University, 1951); BRIT (Charles University); encyclopedia.com (a postgraduate degree from the Czechoslovak Academy of Sciences in 1956). **Disputed (institution and year).** The script names neither.

**F58.** In 1958 he defected at a scientific conference in Israel, then lived in Israel, Britain and, from 1967, Canada.
Sources: WP; encyclopedia.com (Israeli Ministry of Agriculture, 1958–60; UK Medical Research Council, 1960–67); BRIT. **Agreed.**

**F59.** He taught pharmacology at UBC in Vancouver for many years.
Sources: OBIT (Press Democrat/AP: professor of pharmacology at UBC from 1976 into the early 1990s); BRIT; encyclopedia.com (associate professor from 1967). **Disputed (start date).** He moved to Canada in 1967 for the Medical Research Council of Canada, and the UBC appointment is usually dated 1976. The script says "for many years".

**F60.** Wetzler stayed in Czechoslovakia, wrote about Auschwitz under a pen name, and died in Bratislava in 1988.
Sources: WP "Alfréd Wetzler" (died 8 February 1988 in Bratislava; wrote as Jozef Lánik, *Čo Dante nevidel* / *What Dante Did Not See*, 1963); RV. **Agreed.**

**F61.** He testified in a 1963 memoir, in Lanzmann's *Shoah*, and in 1985 in a Toronto court as a witness against a Holocaust denier.
Sources: MEM (*I Cannot Forgive*, 1963, with Alan Bestic); USHMM (Lanzmann *Shoah* outtakes); RV "Vrba vs. Zündel". He was a prosecution witness in the 1985 trial of Ernst Zündel. **Agreed.**

**F62.** Vrba believed Hungary's Jewish leaders kept the report quiet while negotiating with the SS, and that Kasztner "paid for" the lives of nearly 1,700 people on his train "with silence".
Sources: WP; RV "Vrba vs. Kasztner"; MEM. The search sources attribute the line "Kasztner paid for those 1,684 lives with his silence" to Vrba. The train left Budapest on 30 June 1944, and 1,684 is the count registered at Bergen-Belsen. **Agreed** that this was Vrba's view. The script paraphrases instead of quoting until the exact quote is confirmed in a primary source.

**F63.** Many historians reject that judgement and argue the leaders had few real choices. The matter is not settled.
Sources: BAU (calls Vrba's attack "ahistorical"; argues that conditions made publicising the report and escaping impossible, and that it was already too late); GIL ("a dupe not a villain"); on the other side, Hungarian historians cited by RV (e.g. Gergely Kunt) and Erich Kulka. **Agreed** that it is disputed.

**F64.** He died in Vancouver in March 2006, aged 81.
Sources: OBIT (Washington Post: died of cancer on 27 March 2006 in a Vancouver hospital); BRIT. **Agreed.**

**F65.** "Part of it arrived in time. Part of it did not."
**Interpretation**, summarising F50–F53.

## Things deliberately left out or softened

- **"The first Jews to escape Auschwitz."** This is not true as usually stated. Siegfried Lederer escaped Birkenau on 5 April 1944, two days before Vrba and Wetzler went into hiding, and there were earlier attempts. They are better described as the first escapees whose report reached the free world and was believed. The script makes no "first" claim.
- **"Their report saved 200,000 lives."** This is a popular headline, but the causal claim is contested. The script says it was "part of the pressure".
- **Exact quotations from Vrba.** All of his words are paraphrased and attributed ("he recalled", "he believed") until they are checked against MEM. The single quoted phrase, "Hungarian salami", is attributed.
- **Names of helpers** (the prisoners who covered the woodpile, the farmer near Skalité, Dr Pollák in Čadca): they could not be confirmed, so they are left out rather than risk getting them wrong.
- **Page count of the report**: 32, 33 or 40 pages in different sources.
- **Exact number of transports or people Vrba personally counted**: no reliable figure found.
